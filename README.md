# Vhaezrathion

> The furnace was cold. Its ashes burned the hands of those who remembered the fire.

## The Smith Who Forged an Absence

The smith of Vhaezrathion lived beneath a roof of copper leaves. Rain struck the leaves without making a sound. Thunder, however distant, could be heard inside the smallest room, where a black anvil stood upon three pieces of white stone.

No weapon emerged from that workshop. No hinge, nail, or ornament bore the smith's mark. Those who visited brought an empty box and departed with the same box, now too heavy to lift with both hands.

The smith accepted no payment. He asked each visitor to leave one unanswered question beside the furnace. By winter, the questions could be seen rising through the chimney like pale insects. None crossed the edge of the roof.

## A Testimony in Copper

A traveler entered carrying a broken bell. He asked the smith to restore its voice. The smith examined the crack and asked which silence had broken it.

The traveler could not answer. He had found the bell on a road that appeared only after sunset, beside the footprint of a creature too large to have passed between the nearby trees.

The smith placed the bell on the anvil. His hammer remained hanging upon the wall. Nevertheless, three blows were heard, each followed by a brief darkness in which the traveler's reflection stood apart from his body.

When the light returned, the crack was unchanged. The bell had acquired an inscription on its inner surface. The traveler tried to read it and discovered that the letters moved whenever his breath entered the metal.

He took the bell home. That night, every empty box in the village began to ring.

## The Creature beyond the Chimney

The roof's copper leaves were not made by human hands. Each was a scale, beaten thin without losing the faint warmth of the body that had once carried it.

Sometimes that body could be glimpsed above the chimney: a dark dragon turning slowly in a sky without clouds. Its wings cast no shade. Its open mouth contained the shape of a small doorway, beyond which a furnace burned beneath a copper roof.

The villagers kept their eyes lowered on those mornings. They listened instead for the sound of the hammer. When no blows came, they placed their empty boxes outdoors and left the lids open until dusk.

One child left a question in her box. By evening, the question had become a small black stone. It remained warm through every winter that followed.

## The Account of the Three White Stones

The anvil's supports were examined by a mason who believed the workshop rested above a hidden chamber. He tapped each stone and heard three different sounds: water falling, someone laughing, and a bell whose voice had not yet been broken.

He returned with a measuring cord. The cord described the height of the roof correctly, but its knots changed position whenever it approached the anvil. Eventually, all the knots gathered at one end, forming a shape like folded claws.

The mason asked permission to move a stone. The smith offered him an empty box instead.

He refused the box. The next morning, his own house stood upon three white stones, and the smallest room contained a shadow shaped like an anvil. He never entered that room again. His family heard thunder there on clear days.

## Letters That Refuse the Bell

**Vhaez — rath — ion — ekluun.**

These sounds belong to the inscription, though no reader has seen all four at once. The first appears when the bell faces the furnace. The second appears when it faces the road. The third requires an empty box beneath it. The last has been recorded only by someone who was unable to hear.

That witness drew the word as a narrow door. Inside the drawing, a hammer hung upon a wall. Beneath it stood the traveler, holding a bell without a crack.

The smith kept the drawing behind the furnace. Its paper never darkened, even when the ashes were spread across it. The narrow door remained visible after the rest of the page had been folded away.

## The Question Left after Closing

> What did the smith place inside the boxes?
>
> The weight that would not remain upon the anvil.
>
> Why did the questions stay beneath the roof?
>
> Because the sky had already answered elsewhere.
>
> Who struck the bell while the hammer hung still?
>
> Someone whose hands were waiting on the other side of the sound.

The traveler found these answers written in ash outside his house. Their author left no footprints. A single copper leaf lay at the end of the final line, reflecting a furnace with a person seated inside it.

## The Cold Fire

The workshop's furnace has been cold for many years. Its ashes still burn those who recall a flame. The black anvil remains supported by three stones, and the unanswered questions continue gathering beneath the leaves.

The smith is absent from every account written after the winter of the bell. Yet visitors still arrive with empty boxes. They wait before the door until their shadows become too heavy to move.

Vhaezrathion holds the silence between three blows. Above its chimney, a doorway remains open in the mouth of something that has never learned to swallow fire.
